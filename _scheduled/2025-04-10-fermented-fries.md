#### Abstract

A recipe for fries with a fermented tang, reminiscent of vinegar and salt fries.

#### Materials

- Large bowl
- Fermentation container for fries (preferably glass)
- Recommended: Kitchen scale
- Kitchen Towel
- Air fryer

#### Procedure

##### Prepare Potato Sticks
1. Cut potato into even matchsticks about 1/2 x 1/2" and place into a large bowl.
- Cut potatoes as evenly as possible to ensure even cooking.
- Potatoes may be cut thicker or thinner, cooking time will need to be adjusted accordingly.
2. Submerge the sticks in water and gently agitate to wash the sticks.
3. Pour out the starchy water and soak sticks in fresh water. Set aside.

##### Prepare Fermenting Brine
1. In a clean container, measure and pour 500 mL or 500 g of water.
2. Add 2 Tbsp of white vinegar or sauerkraut pickling juice.
3. Add 15 g of salt to make 3% brine. Add additional spices like garlic powder, chili powder, black pepper and sage to taste.

##### Ferment Potato Fries
1. Add soaked potato sticks and fermenting brine to the fermenting container. Ensure sticks are completely submerged.
2. Optional: Add a cabbage leaf to the fermenting container to prevent sticks from floating.
3. Put the lid on loosely to allow gases to escape. Leave fermenting container in a cool, dark place for 3-7 days.
- Tighten the lid and mix contents about once daily. Loosen the lid afterwards.
- By day 3, you should observe some changes in the brine. The brine will appear cloudy and bubbles will float to the top if the jar is tapped. The potatoes are ready to be fried.
- Potato sticks may be left to ferment longer, which will develop a stronger funkiness.

##### Frying Fermented Fries
1. Preheat the air fryer to 375<sup>o</sup>F.
2. Drain the brining liquid and dry the potsto sticks with a towel.
- Brine may be stored in the fridge for up to 1 month to be used as fermentation starter for future batches.
3. Add the sticks to the pre-heated air fryer and spray with cooking oil.
4. Cook for 20-30 mins or until golden brown. Shake the basket and spray with more oil every 10 mins.
5. Optional: After cooking, let the fries cool either in the fridge or freezer for 15 mins. Bake again at 400<sup>o</sup>F for 5 mins.
- This step allows the fries to crisp up even more.

##### Serve Fermented Fries
1. Fries will taste salty, sour and garlicky, so additional toppings are not required.
2. Serve fries with ketchup or mayo.

When tasting my first batch of fermented fries, I was instantly hooked and made several more batches. The potatoes slices are soaked in a flavorful brine which allows the flavors to really soak into the rest of the fry, not just the surface. The lactic acid produced by the fermentation created a subtle flavor which can be picked up in some but not all fries. Some were a little sour, some were downright funky, reminiscent of a stinky cheese. This flavor variance intrigued me and no matter how long I fermented my fries for, (3 vs 7 days) there were still some fries without any hint of funkiness.

##### Fermentation

Fermented fries are not so different from your typical French fries, with the main difference being the fermentation time. To make crispy French fries at home, the potato slices will need to be soaked in water anyway to remove excess starches. However, we add another purpose to this soaking with fermentation - and that is where the magic happens. The bacteria that naturally occurs on the skins of potatoes, cabbage leaves and sauerkraut pickling juice kick starts the fermentation process which produces the cloudy, sour brine after about 3 days. The bacteria break down complex carbohydrates into lactic acid through a process called [Lactic Acid Fermentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactic_acid_fermentation). This acid by-product not only contributes to the unique flavor of fermented fries, but also aids in its preservation, as the acidic, low pH environment is no longer suitable for other bacteria species to grow.

##### Variations

In my take on fermented fries, I leaned heavily into the garlicky flavor profile for the brine. This paid off, as the garlic flavor was very strong after frying. This was inspired by a personal favorite take with garlic sage fries from [Southern Pacific Brewing](https://www.southernpacificbrewing.com/). Other favorite toppings to try adding to the brine are more spices (play around with chili flakes), fresh aromatics (rather than dried) and other herbs. Conversely, a plain brine is more than enough to produce delicious fermented fries.
