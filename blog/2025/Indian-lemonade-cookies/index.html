indian lemonade cookie

Created on June 04, 2025

2025   ·   cookies,   baking,   sweet   ·   recipes

Abstract

A refreshing recipe that combines the zesty classic lemon crinkle cookie with a savory, spiced Indian twist.

Heavily inspired by Sally’s recipe combined with Shikanji Nimbu Pani (Indian lemonade).

Materials

  • (Recommended) Weighing scale
  • Silicone spatula
  • 2 Mixing bowls
  • Large sifter
  • Measuring cups/spoons
  • Cookie scoop (2 Tbsp or other)
  • Freezer bag
  • 2 Large baking sheets
  • Cooling rack
  • Silicone cookie sheet
  • (Optional) Mixer with paddle attachment
  • 2 Bowls

Reagents

  Ingredient Amount Preparation
All-purpose flour 2 and 1/2 cups (310g) Spooned & leveled
Ground (roasted) cumin 2 Tsp None
Amchur powder (dried mango powder) 1 Tsp None
Black pepper 1 Tsp Ground finely
Baking soda 1 Tsp None
Corn starch 1 Tsp May substitute with double volume of potato starch
Kosher salt 1/2 Tsp None
Black salt 1/2 Tsp Optional: substitute with kosher salt
Granulated sugar 1 cup + 1 Tbsp (225g) None
Lemon zest 1 Tbsp None
Unsalted Butter 3/4 cup (16 Tbsp; 226g) Room temperature
Large egg 1 Room temperature
Pure vanilla extract 2 Tsp None
Lemon juice or Indian lemonade 1/4 cup (60mL) None
Optional: Lemon peel candies 1/4 cup None


Optional: Sugar Coating
  Ingredient Amount Preparation
Granulated sugar 3 Tbsp (35g) None
Optional: Kosher salt 1/2 Tsp None
Confectioner’s sugar 1 cup (120g) None


Procedure

Prepare Dry Ingredients

Hands on: 2 mins

  1. In a large bowl, sift flour, ground cumin powder, amchur pwder, black pepper, baking soda, corn starch, salt and black salt. Set aside.
Prepare Wet Ingredients

Hands on: 15 mins | Incubation: 15-20 mins

  1. In mixing a bowl, add granulated sugar and lemon zest.
  2. Mix by hand, squeezing the zest to incorporate citrus oils into the sugar. Set aside.
Combine Ingredients

Hands on: 5 mins

  1. Whisk butter and lemon zest-sugar on medium until combined ~1 min.
  2. Add vanilla, eggs and lemon juice.
  3. Whisk wet ingredients on high until fluffy ~2 mins.
    • Batter will look curdled but that’s ok.
  4. Whisk on low. Add half of dry ingredients until combined. Add the rest of the dry ingredients until combined. Scrape down the sides of the bowl.
  5. Add lemon peel candies, if using. Mix with spatula until combined, ensuring bottom is well-mixed. Dough will be sticky and thick.
Chill Dough

Hands-on: 10 mins | Incubation: 3-24 h

  1. Cover mixing bowl and place in the fridge for 1-2 hours.
  2. Scoop cookies and place into a freezer bag. Let the dough rest for 2-24 hours.
Optional: Sugar Coating
  1. Before baking, in 2 separate bowls, add granulated sugar and confectioner’s sugar. Add salt to the bowl with granulated sugar if using and mix thoroughly.
  2. Roll cookie dough in granulated sugar in an even coating.
  3. Roll the sugar-coated cookie in confectioner’s sugar in an even coating. Roll 2 or 3 times for a thicker coating.
Bake

Incubation: 22-29 mins

  1. Pre-heat the oven and bake at 350oF for 12-14 mins until bottom edges are golden brown.
    • Space cookies with 2” between each to allow room for spreading.
    • Time will depend on factors such as cookie scoop size and texture preference. Try 13 mins first, then adjust from there.
  2. Allow cookies to cool on the baking sheet for 10-15 mins before moving to wire rack.
  3. Serve warm!
Yield 20-24 using a 2-Tbsp cookie scoop
Storage Cookies stay fresh for 3 days on the counter or 1 week in the fridge. Microwave for ~7s before enjoying.
Extra dough may be stored in the freezer for up to 6 months. When baking from frozen, pre-heat the oven to 350oF and add a minute to the bake time.


Discussion

Lemon cookies. Photo from Unsplash.

For a picnic, we made Indian lemonade from Ashley and had a ton of leftover lemons and lemon zest! After the initial confusion of tasting a salted drink, it really grows on you as a refreshing summer pick-me-up. This spiced lemonade inspired my take on a savory lemon crinkle (optional) cookie recipe.

Spice Substitutions

Some of the spices may be hard to source without a local Indian grocery store and cannot be substituted easily. Powders will usually come in bulk packages, but luckily these spices are versatile and can be incorporated into every day cooking dishes.

Cumin powder is earthy and nutty with a strong aroma. Some distant substitutes are caraway seeds, ground coriander and surprisingly, taco seasoning which uses a blend containing cumin.

Amchur powder has a tangy, familiar profile owing to its mango content. This powder is used more for the sour taste than the flavor, so substitution is more flexible. Some close substitutions are extra lemon juice, tamarind paste, sumac or chaat masala.

Black salt has an earthy, mineral flavor that cannot be replicated with table salt. I highly recommend sourcing some from an Indian grocery store if possible.
