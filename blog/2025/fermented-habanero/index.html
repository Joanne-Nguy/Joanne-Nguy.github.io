fermented habanero sauce

Created on April 23, 2025

2025   ·   ferments   ·   recipes

Abstract

A recipe for a fiery, fruity fermented hot sauce with a touch of tart plum and smokey paprika — perfect for adding heat and complexity to your dishes

Materials

  • Recommended: food-safe powder-free gloves
  • Knife and cutting board
  • Small bowl and spoon
  • Sterilized jar
  • Pickling weight or cabbage leaf
  • Plate with rim
  • Strainer and bowl
  • Food processor or blender
  • Bottle with pour spout

Reagents

  Ingredient Amount Preparation
Habanero peppers 3/4-1 Cup Washed
Carrot 1 Carrot Washed, cut into matchsticks
Shallots or yellow onion 1/2 Cup Diced
Garlic 4-8 Cloves Sliced
(Filtered) Water 2 Cups None
Kosher salt 14 g None
Optional: Sauerkraut pickling juice 2 Tbsp None
Recommended: Plum 1/2 Plum Wash and skin peeled
Optional: Salt To taste None
Optional: Smoked paprika To taste None


Procedure

Prepare Habaneros

Hands-on: 5 mins

  1. Don gloves, wash gloves with soap.
  2. Using a knife and cutting board, slice habaneros in half. Remove seeds and stem.
  3. Slice habaneros into thin strips - the thinner, the faster the ferment.
  4. Set aside in a small bowl. Discard gloves.
Prepare Pickling Brine

Hands-on: 5 mins

  1. Add salt to the water to make a 3% brine.
  2. In a jar, add carrot matchsticks, diced shallots and sliced garlic. Using a spoon, add the sliced habaneros to the jar.
  3. Top off with brine, making sure all vegetables are submerged.
  4. Add the pickling weight or cabbage leaf to prevent veggies from floating.
  5. Fill rest of the jar with the brine. Add sauerkraut pickling juice if using.
Ferment

Incubation: 7-60 days

  1. Place jar in a cool, dark spot over a plate with a rim.
  2. Close the lid partway to allow fermentation gases to escape. Ferment for 7-60 days.
    • Bubbles should start to form a day after fermenting if using sauerkraut pickling juice as a starter or within 3 days.
    • Longer fermentation mellows the spice in the habaneros.
Blend Sauce

Hands-on: 10 mins

  1. Strain the fermented vegetables over a bowl to reserve the pickling liquid.
  2. Add the fermented vegetables minus the cabbage to a food processor or blender. Add 1/4 to 1 cup of pickling liquid to the food processor depending on preferred thickness of the sauce.
  3. Add plum, smoked paprika, salt and any additional toppings to taste.
  4. Blend vegetables until a smooth sauce forms.
  5. Add sauce to sterilized glass jar or bottle with an open pour spout.
  6. Store in the fridge loosely covered as sauce will continue to ferment.
Yield 14 oz.
Storage Store in the fridge for up to 3 months. Keep loosely covered.


Discussion

Habaneros fresh from the market. Photo from Unsplash.

I made this fermented habanero hot sauce as a way to enjoy a batch of fresh habaneros from the market in an enjoyable way. I fermented my batch for just a couple days under a month. Once fermented, the initial taste was tangy and bright up front, with a manageable, lingering heat. The sauce was also surprisingly sweet, but I wanted to add a bit of tartness with some plums that were on sale and smokiness with a pinch of paprika.

If you love this sauce, check out the yuzu habanero jelly for another creative twist.

Fermentation

The bacteria that are found on the peels of root vegetables like carrots and potatoes or leafy vegetables like cabbage are responsible for starting the fermentation process. Those bacteria are dormant in sauerkraut pickling juice, but can be reactivated when taken out of the cold of the fridge and provided carbohydrates from the fresh vegetables through a process called Lactic Acid Fermentation. The fermentation process also moderates the spice of the habaneros though will not completely remove it.

Variations

The usual habanero pairing is mango or strawberry, a sweet, tart fruit to contrast the burning spice of habanero. I had plums on hand and found the flesh to be refreshing and slightly tart. Feel free to get creative with whatever fruit you’ve got lying around! Other pairings are kiwi, pineapple and pomegranate.

In addition to the fruit added after fermentation, we added carrots to the pickle to help jump start the fermentation with the bacteria usually found on carrot skin. The mildness of carrots will also balance the spiciness of the habanero. Other neutral vegetables can be used in place of carrot such as bell peppers.

Spice Level

Habaneros bring serious heat, but with fermentation and fruity additions, they become surprisingly approachable. These peppers have a rating of 100-350k Scoville Heat Units (SCU) which is on the spicier side, since it was readily available to me for free at a farmer’s market. The resulting heat in the jelly was surprisingly bearable (coming from someone with a mild spice tolerance). Fermenting the peppers will reduce the heat, so the fermenting time may be adjusted based on desired spice level. Yes, my tongue and mouth still burned, but it was nothing like the heat I felt after slicing the habaneros, washing my hands (3 times!) and touching my face - please be careful when handling peppers!

For comparison, here are the SCUs of some commonly eaten peppers:

Pepper Spiciness (SCU)
Bell pepper 0
Jalapeno pepper 2.5-10k
Cayenne pepper 25-50K
Thai chilies 50-100k
Habanero pepper 100k-350K
