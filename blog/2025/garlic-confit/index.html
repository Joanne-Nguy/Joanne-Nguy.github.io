garlic confit sauce (mojo de ajo)

Created on April 21, 2025

2025   ·   cooking,   savory   ·   recipes

Abstract

A recipe for spreadable liquid gold - garlic confit. The confit process, or low-heat frying, allows the flavors to develop as the garlic cloves fry into a golden brown.

Materials

  • Oven safe pan
  • Blender or food processor
  • 3 Sterilized glass jars

Reagents

  Ingredient Amount Preparation
Garlic 50 cloves, about 4 heads, or enough to cover the bottom of the pan Peeled
Olive oil 1-2 Cups, or enough to cover the garlic cloves None
Optional: Fresh Rosemary 3-5 Sprigs Washed and dried
Optional: Fresh Thyme 3-5 Sprigs Washed and dried
Optional: Black peppercorn 3-5 Peppercorns None
Optional: Chili flakes 1 Tbsp None
An acid (lemon juice, orange juice or vinegar) 1/2 cup None
Salt 1/2 Tbsp None
Optional: Fresh Cilantro 1 Cup Washed and dried


Procedure

Confit the Garlic

Hands-on: 10 mins | Incubation: 1-1.5 h

  1. Preheat oven to 300oF.
  2. Add garlic cloves to the pan in a single layer.
  3. Add toppings if using.
  4. Add olive oil until all cloves and toppings are submerged.
  5. Bake at 300oF for 1-1.5h or until garlic is golden brown.
  6. Serve the fresh garlic confit and oil spread over toast or proteins.
Make the Garlic Sauce

Hands-on: 5 mins

  1. Add the garlic, your acid, salt and any toppings to a blender. Mixture will be thick.
  2. Stream in the reserved olive oil until desired consistency.
    • Excess oil may be stored in an air-tight container in the fridge for up to 1 month
  3. Serve the garlic sauce over toast or proteins.
Yield 2-3 10 oz jars.
Storage Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 1 month or in the freezer for up to 3 months. Sauce will separate and oil layer may solidify, so scoop from both layers.


Discussion

Garlic cloves sizzling in a pan with olive oil. Photo from Unsplash.

The sauce is a perfect addition to toast or any strongly-flavored protein dish. With the pungent initial bite of garlic, followed by mellow notes of the added herbs and spices, this sauce is a garlic-lover’s dream. A flavorful oil is also produced, which may be drizzled over pastas or soups.

Safety

This recipe is a way of preserving large amounts of garlic cloves for long-term storage (thank you Costco-sized bag of peeled garlic). Garlic stored in oil has a short shelf life due to the anaerobic, low-acid environment which is ideal for Clostridium botulinum to reproduce. I adapt the Brazilian mojo de ajo recipe with its use of an acid to prevent the ideal environment for C. botulinum. This adds a bright splash of flavor to complement the caramelized garlic flavor of the confit. Feel free to use any citrus on hand - I personally love the combination of orange juice and yuzu extract. The prepared garlic sauce has a long shelf-life in the refrigerator, though it will disappear very quickly!

Variations

Garlic is an ingredient that it used in dishes worldwide. The toppings added to the oil help develop more flavor as it confits and that flavor profile can be changed to suit your dish. I opted for a bright and herbal sauce that works well with a variety of dishes - my personal favorites being sandwiches and steaks. The sauce can be made spicier with the addition of chili flakes, ginger and cinnamon. Or another variation could be more suitable for pastas with the addition of fresh oregano and basil.

Olive oil is used to impart a light profile, but heavier animal fats can be used as well to create a smokier oil. I have tried this recipe using leftover fat from frying Chinese sausage, lap cheong, and adding the rest of the volume in olive oil to submerge the garlic cloves. This produced a sauce with complex layers of flavor which can’t be achieved using olive oil alone.
