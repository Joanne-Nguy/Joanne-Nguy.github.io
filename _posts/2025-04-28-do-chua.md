#### Abstract

A recipe for refreshingly crisp Vietnamese pickled carrot and daikon side dish.

Inspired by [Hungry Huy's recipe](https://www.hungryhuy.com/vietnamese-pickled-daikon-carrot-recipe-do-chua/#wprm-recipe-container-3453).

#### Materials

- Colander
- [Optional](https://www.hungryhuy.com/vietnamese-pickled-daikon-carrot-recipe-do-chua/#wprm-recipe-container-3453): Paper towels or cheese cloth
- Mandolin or vegetable peeler
- Saucepan
- Measuring cups
- 2 26 oz Sterilized glass jars

#### Procedure

##### Prepare Vegetables
**Hands-on**: 30 mins | **Incubation**: 15 mins
1. **Optional**: Line a colander with paper towels or cheese cloth.
2. Cut carrots and daikon into matchsticks using a mandolin or vegetable peeler. Thinner cuts will pickle faster.
3. Place matchsticks in the colander.
4. Sprinkle salt over the veggies and toss to evenly coat. Let sit for 15 mins to pull moisture out of the veggies.
5. Rinse vegetables to remove salt.
6. Squeeze vegetables with cheese cloth if using to remove excess moisture.
7. Add vegetables to jars.

##### Prepare Pickling Solution
**Hands-on**: 10 mins
1. Boil water in a saucepan.
2. Add vinegar and sugar, stirring until sugar dissolves.
3. Pour pickling solution in the jars with vegetables evenly.
4. **Optional**: Add filtered water to submerge the veggies.

##### Ferment Do Chua
**Incubation**: 2-5 days
1. Screw on jar lids tightly.
- As the contents of the jar cools, the jar lid will pop to indicate that a seal has formed. Seal may be tested by pushing the button on the lid - if it pops, seal is compromised.
2. Store jars in a cool, dark spot for 2-5 days.
3. Bubbles will start to form after 1-2 days.
4. Store jar in the fridge 1 day after bubbles form.
5. Serve on the side or as a topping to main dishes.

As a refreshing and versatile Vietnamese side dish, do chua can be found in many dishes which feature rich or heavy ingredients to balance the flavors. Do chua literally translates to "sour stuff" funnily enough. The sweet, satisfying crunch of the pickled veggies along with the fragrant, slightly tangy pickling juice, makes this one of my favorite side dishes. Initially, this recipe was prepared for long-term storage of a carrot harvest and evolved into a tradition, where a fresh batch is always found in the fridge.

##### Main dishes

The first dish I made with my fresh pickled carrots and radish was summer rolls. Using a stretchy circle of rice paper, fresh ingredients are wrapped -- including do chua for a tangy crunch. As a rule of thumb, anywhere fish sauce is used, do chua can be used to either contrast the strong flavor or even added to the sauce to brighten it.

Other dishes I've made to go with the do chua are banh mi (stuffed sandwich with a rich slathering of liver pate and butter), banh xeo (savory crispy crepes with pork belly and shrimp), bo tai chanh (tangy and pungent lemon-marinated beef carpaccio) and bun thit nuong (vermicelli noodles with BBQ pork). These dishes all revolve around a fatty ingredient, where the do chua helps lighten the flavor and mouthfeel.
