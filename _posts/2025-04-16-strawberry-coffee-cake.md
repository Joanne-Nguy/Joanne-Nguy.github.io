#### Abstract

A recipe for a dense coffee cake with swirls of cinnamon, pockets of fresh strawberries topped with gooey, salty cheese.

Heavily inspired by [Sally's](https://sallysbakingaddiction.com/blackberry-cream-cheese-crumb-cake/) recipe with some substitutions. I used non-fat greek yogurt instead of full-fat sour cream; added salt to the cheese topping instead of sugar; opted for no icing.

#### Materials

- 3 Large bowls
- Cling wrap
- Colander
- Recommended: Stand mixer with paddle attachment
- Silicone spatula
- **Optional**: Angled spatula
- 9" Round springform pan
- 9" Round parchment paper
- Aluminum foil to cover 9" springform pan
- BBQ skewer

#### Procedure

##### Prepare Crumb Topping
**Hands-on**: 5 mins
1. In a large bowl, combine brown sugar, flour and cinnamon.
2. Add the cold butter and press between fingers to create small flat disks of butter. Continue pressing the butter until pea-sized crumbs remain.
3. Cover the bowl and place in fridge to chill.

##### Prepare Cheese Topping
**Hands-on**: 5 mins
1. In a large bowl, add the softened cheese and salt.
2. Whisk until combined. The cheese will be thick.

##### Prepare Berries
**Hands-on**: 5 mins
1. If using fresh berries, wash in a colander and briefly dry.
2. Cut the berries into 1/2 x 1/2" cubes.
- The size does not need to be precise. At this size, strawberries will still have a slight crunch after baking.
3. Set prepared berries aside.

##### Prepare Batter
**Hands-on**: 10 mins
1. In a large bowl, sift flour, baking powder, baking soda and salt. Briefly mix with a silicone spatula. Set dry ingredients aside.
2. In a stand mixer, ddd butter and granulated sugar. Mix on high until creamy, about 2 mins.
3. Add 1 egg at a time, mixing until fully incorporated.
4. Add vanilla extract and greek yogurt, mixing briefly.
5. Scrape down the sides of the bowl.
6. On low, add half of the dry ingredients until incorporated. Add the other half of the dry ingredients. Mix until incorporated.
7. Add prepared berries and fold in with silicone spatula.

##### Bake Cake
**Hands-on**: 10 mins | **Incubation**: 1.5 h
1. Pre-heat oven at 350<sup>o</sup>F and grease 9" springform pan.
2. In the greased springform pan, spread about 1/2 to 2/3 of the cake batter to cover the bottom of the pan with the angled spatula, if using.
3. Sprinkle half the crumb on top of the batter
4. Spread the rest of the cake batter. 
- Batter will be thick and difficult to spread, but when baked, it will spread to form a complete layer.
5. Spread cheese topping. Again, cheese will be thick, but will spread with heat.
6. Sprinkle the rest of the crumb layer over the cheese topping.
7. Bake cake at 350<sup>o</sup>F for 60-65 mins or until center no longer jiggles. Halfway through bake time, lightly wrap aluminum foil over the springform pan. 
- Test cake doneness with a BBQ skewer through the center of the cake -- it should come out mostly clean.
8. Cool cake for at least 30 mins at room temperature before removing the springform pan.
9. Serve generous slices with tea or coffee.

When I found strawberries half-off at my local grocery store, I knew I needed to bake a strawberry cake. The sourness of the discounted strawberries complemented the sweet batter, the subtle spiciness of the cinnamon swirls and the saltiness of the cheese topping. The cake is dense but very moist, owing to the addition of greek yogurt (my favorite ingredient to look for in a cake recipe!). This recipe uses readily available ingredients in my pantry and I encourage you to as well, by understanding how certain substitutions affect the cake chemistry and overall flavor profile.

##### Variations

The berries used in my recipe were fresh strawberries, since they were readily available. Feel free to substitute with other berries, fresh or otherwise, such as blackberries, raspberries, blueberries or cherries. Dry ingredients such as chocolate chips, dried fruit or nuts may be added as well for more texture.

I used mascarpone since I had it on hand (as leftover from a rich tiramisu recipe). The original recipe used cream cheese, which is more economical and lower in calories, but calls for the addition of sugar when whipping. The mascarpone is a cheese that is already sweet, so I opted to add salt to balance the flavor. The cheese ended up being a thicker layer on top of the cake which added pockets of saltiness to balance the sweetness of the cake and sourness of the strawberries. Consider complementing your cheese topping to your fruit addition!

The crumb topping was a simple brown sugar and cinnamon combo, but other warm spices can be added in addition to the cinnamon such as, cardamom, allspice and nutmeg. For this recipe, I wanted to keep it simple so my star ingredient, the strawberries, would shine.
